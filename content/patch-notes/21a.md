The single player campaign is finally here! 28 missions across three factions, plus a new Teams Mode setting for maps and a handful of balance changes.

Back in the 20f notes I said the next project was expanding the tutorial into something "more like a prelude, or a cross between a story primer & tutorial." That project grew into a full campaign! (It's also the reason this update took as long as it did.)

## Campaign

There are **28 missions** in total, split across three separate storylines:

- **Humans** -- 12 missions
- **Robots** -- 8 missions
- **Critters** -- 8 missions

To play, press **New Game**, then **Campaign**, then pick a faction. Progress saves automatically, so you can put it down mid-campaign and pick it back up whenever.

Each campaign starts from the very beginning -- moving a unit, taking cover -- and introduces units and mechanics one at a time until you're playing something close to a full battle. The Human campaign is the longest and the most tutorial-ish, and you have to play the first few missions of it before the Robot and Critter campaigns are unlocked. The Robot and Critter campaigns assume you already know how to play and get to the interesting stuff faster.

The later missions in each faction are meant to be a real fight, not a walkthrough. If you've been playing for years and you breeze through all 28, let me know! The campaign is meant to be *somewhat* of a challenge, even for experienced players. (I may eventually add a 'Hard Mode' to the campaign later on, for veteran players only.)

> Note: the campaign is very much a **work in progress**! It's a lot of new content all at once, and I'm relying on players like you to tell me what's working and what's not.
>
> Specifically, I'd love feedback on the difficulty, length, and story of each mission.

As always, please bring up bug reports and feedback in [Discord](https://discord.gg/h5C5wEx)! Your feedback helps improve the game for everyone else.

## Teams Mode for Maps

Maps now have a **Teams Mode** setting, sitting alongside the other map settings in the map editor.

This exists because maps built for team play - the ones where allied bases sit side by side - made for pretty cramped free-for-all games. If you've ever started an FFA on a 2v2 map and found yourself spawning right next to an enemy while someone else got a quiet corner, this is the fix.

- Set it to **Free-For-All** (the default) if the bases are far enough apart for an FFA game.
- Set it to a specific teams mode (`2v2`, `2v1`, `3v3`, `2v2v2`) if the spawn layout only makes sense for that mode.

Maps set to a teams mode **will no longer show up for free-for-all games**. It still works the other way around: FFA maps remain available for team games, since spread-out bases are fine either way.

2 player maps are unaffected -- those are always 1v1, so the setting is disabled for them.

I've gone through and updated the existing maps that were obviously built for teams. If you've made team-oriented maps of your own, now's a good time to check what mode they're set to.

## Escalating Turn Timeouts

Until now, the turn timeout was a flat **24 hours** for everybody, on every single turn. Which is fine right up until someone stops playing -- and then their opponents pay a full day of waiting for each remaining turn of the game, over and over.

The timeout now escalates per player, per game, based on how many times that player has been skipped:

- Never skipped -- `24 hours` (unchanged)
- Skipped once -- `12 hours`
- Skipped twice or more -- **immediately skippable**, the moment their turn starts

The timeout resets the instant that player submits a turn of their own. So if you time out once because you were asleep, just take your next turn normally and you'll be straight back to the full 24 hours. Life happens, and this system is not meant to punish the occasional timeout, just to help in cases where someone has completely left.

Once a player has reached that immediately-skippable stage, the skip dialog also offers a new **"Always skip this player"** checkbox. Tick it and that player is skipped for the rest of the game without anybody having to press the button each turn. Players in that state get an indicator in the game list and the game details screen, so it's obvious what's happening. They also stop receiving a "you were skipped" notification every turn.

As before, all of this only applies to non-ranked games -- timed out players in ranked online games can still be kicked.

## Balance Changes

### Pistols for support units

- **Medic**
    - `Weapon: Unarmed -> Pistol`
- **Defragmenter**
    - `Weapon: Unarmed -> Pistol`

Both of these units were stuck throwing punches, which meant that once they'd used their ability, standing anywhere near the fight was the only way to contribute anything at all -- and standing near the fight is exactly what a support unit shouldn't be doing. Now they've each got a `Pistol`.

They're still not meant to win fights on their own. But a support unit should have *something* to do on the turns when its ability is on cooldown, and now they do.

### Omnibus: Place Teleporter rework

- **Omnibus**
    - `Place Teleporter`: a single placed teleporter now warps any unit stepping on it to a square adjacent to Omnibus.

`Place Teleporter` had an awkward problem: it did absolutely nothing until the second teleporter was down. You'd spend a turn placing the first one and just... hope nobody messed with it before you could place the other.

Now the first teleporter is useful on its own. Anything stepping on it gets pulled to a square next to Omnibus. Place a second one and they link up into a normal two-way teleporter, exactly as before.

Worth keeping in mind: this works for *enemies* too. A lone teleporter is now an invitation for the other team to land right on top of your Omnibus, so think about where you're leaving it.

### Gunslinger: Haste

- **Gunslinger**
    - `Haste: +2 temporary movement -> temporary movement equal to Gunslinger's full movement (+4)`

Haste has always been a bit of a non-ability. Two extra movement points, in exchange for your action or a Ready Attack, is a bit underwhelming.

Making it a full refill of Gunslinger's movement should make it more useful: shoot, run all the way back out of range, and live to shoot again next turn. In addition to hit-and-run tactics, it could also be used for a quick reposition after respawning to get back into a faraway fight.

(As before, these extra movement points do not come back upon getting a kill.)

### Firewall: Throw Oil Barrel

- **Firewall**
    - `Throw Oil Barrel range: 4 -> 5`

This reverts the range nerf from 20a. Sometimes the more balanced thing is less fun `:)`

## Help Text Improvements

- New: **terrain help** for cover, walls, and pits. Tap them to see a description, and tap again to dismiss. This is mainly intended to help new players get oriented on the map
- Fighter **immunities** are showing up in the help text again
- `Teleport Gun` (Architect) now correctly mentions in its description that it deals **2 damage**
- Flavor text has been written for the units that were missing it: `Private`, `Space Cadet`, `Minion`, `Task Manager`, `Virus`, `Housefly`, `Grunt`, and `Hooligan`. Check the Unit Almanac to read up on where your favorite cannon fodder comes from!
